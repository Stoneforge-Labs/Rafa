# **Purpose and operating doctrine**

This playbook is the central source of truth for how the company makes decisions and conducts work across every department. It converts the technical platform into an enduring business: choosing markets, authorizing projects, selling and pricing work, staffing teams, controlling cost and quality, protecting rights, managing clients, learning from delivery, and scaling without losing accountability.

**Management doctrine:** The company scales by proving repeatability at each stage. Revenue, headcount, infrastructure, and model ownership are not ends by themselves. Capital is released when quality, reliability, customer value, rights readiness, and unit economics meet predefined gates.

# **1\. Company identity, strategy, and seven-year destination**

## **1.1 Mission**

Create a trusted software-defined production company that transforms approved ideas into consistent, traceable, rights-aware media while retaining the creative judgment, technical knowledge, and verified execution data needed to improve every subsequent project.

## **1.2 Strategic thesis**

* Creative quality and production repeatability improve when artistic intent is converted into governed, versioned production contracts rather than passed through informal handoffs.  
* Deterministic structural rendering reduces geometry, camera, timing, and identity drift; generative systems are most valuable when used as controlled enhancement rather than the sole source of structure.  
* A durable business advantage comes from workflow knowledge, rights-safe asset libraries, evaluation systems, cost data, and operational reliability—not merely from access to the same external models available to competitors.  
* A modular provider strategy protects the company from vendor changes and enables measured migration to local inference when economics and quality justify it.  
* A small, senior, cross-functional team can create more value than a large undifferentiated team when roles, interfaces, acceptance criteria, and automation are explicit.

## **1.3 Seven-year destination**

| Dimension | Year-7 destination |
| :---- | :---- |
| Market position | Recognized specialist in high-consistency, traceable AI-assisted/virtual production for selected customer segments and owned productions. |
| Operating system | Standardized discovery, production, release, archive, and learning processes used across all projects and departments. |
| Platform | Rafa functions as an internal production platform with provider-neutral services, governed data, capacity management, and customer-facing interfaces where valuable. |
| Portfolio | Balanced mix of repeatable service offers, strategic partnerships, and owned/licensed intellectual property. |
| Economics | Project and product decisions are based on measured contribution margin, utilization, quality, cycle time, retention, and capital efficiency. |
| Workforce | Clear career architecture and management system; specialists can onboard and reach defined proficiency within twelve months. |
| Data advantage | Rights-safe asset, evaluation, and execution datasets support better routing, automation, quality prediction, and selective local models. |
| Resilience | Multi-project operations continue through provider outages, worker failures, staff turnover, and tested recovery scenarios. |
| Governance | Board/executive reporting, legal/rights controls, security, privacy, and financial discipline are integrated into routine operations. |

## **1.4 Strategic guardrails**

* Do not accept work when rights, delivery specifications, approval authority, or budget are materially ambiguous.  
* Do not customize the platform for one client without a product, strategic, or clearly priced rationale.  
* Do not promise fully autonomous production; disclose human approval, model variability, and version-dependent capabilities.  
* Do not scale headcount or fixed infrastructure ahead of demonstrated demand and workload thresholds.  
* Do not use client or performer material for model training or unrelated purposes without explicit documented permission.  
* Do not let a single provider, customer, employee, or hardware node become an unmitigated existential dependency.  
* Do not treat gross revenue as success when contribution margin, cash conversion, quality, or retention deteriorate.

# **2\. Business model, offer ladder, pricing, and commercial controls**

## **2.1 Customer problem selection**

The company should begin with use cases where consistency, traceability, version control, structured iteration, or repeated asset reuse matter enough to justify the operating system. Low-value one-off content with no reuse, unclear rights, and extreme price sensitivity should not define the initial business.

| Selection factor | Strong fit | Weak fit / caution |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Consistency | Recurring characters, environments, campaigns, episodic or franchise work. | Single disposable clip where drift is acceptable. |
| Traceability | Regulated, brand-controlled, licensed, or approval-heavy production. | Anonymous speculative content with no approval owner. |
| Iteration value | Multiple revisions benefit from preserved structure and lineage. | Buyer expects unlimited unpriced revisions. |
| Asset reuse | Libraries of characters, locations, cameras, styles, and performance. | All inputs are unique and non-reusable. |
| Delivery requirements | Clear technical specifications and professional post-production. | Undefined platform-only output with no acceptance standard. |
| Economics | Buyer values reliability, speed, rights control, or repeat volume. | Buyer compares solely to lowest-cost commodity generation. |

## **2.2 Offer ladder**

| Offer | Scope | Buyer value | Commercial model |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Offer 0 \- Discovery and production architecture | Paid workshop, feasibility, rights and workflow plan, pilot scope. | Reduces ambiguity; qualifies customer and use case. | Fixed fee with defined deliverables. |
| Offer 1 \- Atomic proof | One governed shot or short scene proving character, camera, environment, polish, and delivery. | Demonstrates capability and establishes benchmark. | Fixed fee; tightly bounded revisions. |
| Offer 2 \- Pilot package | Small sequence or campaign unit with documented approvals, variants, QC, and delivery. | Tests repeatability, customer workflow, and economics. | Milestone-based fixed fee or capped time/materials. |
| Offer 3 \- Repeat production service | Scheduled production capacity, reusable assets, service levels, versioned deliveries. | Predictable output and process integration. | Retainer/capacity reservation plus usage. |
| Offer 4 \- Platform-assisted enterprise workflow | Integration, governance, customer portal/API, custom rights and reporting. | Embeds Rafa capabilities in customer operations. | Implementation \+ subscription/support \+ usage. |
| Offer 5 \- Owned/co-produced IP | Company invests in or licenses content using the platform. | Long-term upside and reusable assets. | Project finance, licensing, distribution, revenue share. |

## **2.3 Pricing procedure**

1. Confirm scope unit: deliverable, shot, finished minute, campaign unit, capacity block, integration milestone, or subscription capability.  
2. Estimate direct labor by role and stage, including management, review, revisions, QC, and support.  
3. Estimate local compute, cloud compute, provider usage, storage, data transfer, software, external specialists, and rights/licensing.  
4. Apply uncertainty and revision contingency based on maturity, content risk, customer responsiveness, and provider variability.  
5. Calculate target contribution margin and cash requirements. Flag negative or unmeasured components.  
6. Define included revisions, review deadlines, cancellation, change-order rates, overage rules, data retention, and rights scope.  
7. Validate against market value and strategic rationale; discount only with named approval and explicit consideration received.  
8. Record the pricing version and assumptions in the opportunity and project forecast; compare estimate to actual after delivery.

### **Pricing worksheet**

| Cost/price element | Method | Owner | Required evidence |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Labor | Loaded internal rate or approved contractor rate x planned hours. | Producer/Finance | Role estimate and delivery plan. |
| Compute | Expected runtime x measured rate by resource class. | FinOps/Engineering | Benchmark and price version. |
| Provider API | Estimated units x provider price \+ retry/variation contingency. | AI Platform/FinOps | Capability and pricing registry. |
| Storage/transfer | Asset volume x retention/tier/egress assumptions. | Infrastructure/FinOps | Storage forecast. |
| Rights/external | Contracted license, performer, music, counsel, specialist costs. | Rights/Producer | Quote or contract assumption. |
| Risk contingency | Risk-weighted reserve with release rules. | Producer/Finance | Risk register. |
| Contribution margin | (Price \- variable/direct costs) / Price. | Finance | Approved target by offer. |
| Payment terms | Deposit, milestone, acceptance, retainer, or usage cycle. | Finance/Sales | Cash-flow model. |

## **2.4 Commercial approval thresholds**

| Decision | Default authority | Escalation |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Standard offer within approved pricing and terms | Sales/Producer within delegated limit. | Finance review for margin/cash exceptions. |
| Discount | Commercial leader within published band. | Executive approval above band; record rationale. |
| Unlimited or open-ended revision language | Prohibited. | Executive \+ legal exception with premium and controls. |
| IP ownership transfer or broad work-for-hire | Legal and executive review. | Board/investor review if strategic IP affected. |
| Use of client data for training or improvement | Explicit separate written permission required. | Privacy/legal approval; no implied consent. |
| High-risk/restricted-personal assets | Rights/privacy approval before proposal commitment. | Executive risk acceptance if legally permissible. |
| Payment terms beyond standard | Finance approval. | Executive approval above delegated exposure. |
| Strategic loss leader | CEO approval with written objective, cap, and review date. | Stop automatically at cap absent renewal. |

## **3.4 Decision protocol**

9. Frame the decision, deadline, decision owner, and consequence of no decision.  
10. Separate facts, assumptions, constraints, and preferences.  
11. Present at least two viable options when the decision is material.  
12. Describe financial, technical, rights, security, customer, people, and reversibility impacts.  
13. Identify who recommends, who decides, who must be consulted, and who is informed.  
14. Record the decision and rationale; define review/expiry condition for reversible decisions.  
15. Translate the decision into changes to requirements, plans, budgets, policies, or work items.

# **4\. Organization design, role architecture, and workforce planning**

## **4.1 Organization design principles**

* Organize around enduring accountabilities and system boundaries, not temporary tools or one project.  
* Keep decision rights close to expertise while preserving a clear accountable owner.  
* Managers are responsible for staffing, priorities, quality, development, and operating health—not merely task assignment.  
* Contractors may supply specialized capacity, but ownership of core architecture, data, security, client commitments, and release remains internal.  
* Every critical role has a deputy or documented continuity plan before the company depends on continuous service.  
* Headcount is released by workload and maturity gates; a job description alone is not justification to hire.

## **4.2 Workforce planning procedure**

16. Translate annual outcomes into capabilities and accountable functions.  
17. Forecast workload by production stage, project class, and service capacity; identify peak and base load.  
18. Assess current proficiency, availability, single points of failure, and succession risk.  
19. Choose among process change, automation, training, internal transfer, contractor, partner, or employee hire.  
20. Create a role scorecard with outcomes, competencies, authority, interfaces, first-year milestones, and manager capacity.  
21. Confirm budget, timing, recruiting capacity, equipment, software, workspace, and onboarding owner before opening the role.  
22. Review hiring plan monthly and cancel/resequence roles when demand, cash, or strategy changes.

## **4.3 Manager span and layer rules**

| Stage | Default pattern | Caution |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Founding team | Senior individual contributors with clear functional leaders; minimal hierarchy. | Founder bottleneck and ambiguous ownership. |
| 10-25 people | Managers lead coherent functions or production pods; generally 4-8 direct reports depending on complexity. | Promoting without management capability. |
| 25-60 people | Dedicated engineering/studio/operations/people/finance leadership; program and production management. | Duplicate coordination layers. |
| 60+ people | Business units or product lines only when differentiated customers/economics justify them. | Fragmented platform and inconsistent standards. |

## **8.3 Risk review**

97. Identify risks from portfolio, customers, cash, rights, security, providers, people, capacity, operations, and reputation.  
98. Score impact, likelihood, velocity, detectability, and control effectiveness.  
99. Assign one accountable owner and a dated treatment plan; avoid committee ownership.  
100. Define trigger, early indicators, contingency, communication, and decision authority.  
101. Escalate high residual risks and expired mitigations to executive leadership.  
102. Close only when evidence confirms treatment or risk acceptance; retain history.
