# **Purpose and principles**

This playbook defines the exact process for deciding that work requires a hire, recruiting and evaluating candidates, making a lawful and evidence-based selection, preparing the organization, and integrating the employee over twelve months. It is intended to produce employees who understand the company, can perform safely within their role, collaborate across the production system, own measurable outcomes, and continue developing after the initial ninety days.

**Onboarding doctrine:** Onboarding is a one-year operating process, not a first-day orientation. The company, manager, buddy, functional experts, and employee each own explicit deliverables. Access, training, production authority, and autonomy expand only after evidence-based proficiency gates.

# **1\. Workforce planning and role authorization**

## **1.1 Hiring decision hierarchy**

1. Define the business outcome, production obligation, risk, or capability gap—not a preferred job title.  
2. Quantify expected workload, duration, criticality, timing, peak/base load, and cost of delay.  
3. Evaluate alternatives in order: stop/defer work, simplify process, automate, train, redeploy, contract/partner, then employee hire.  
4. Confirm which accountabilities must remain internal because they control architecture, data, rights, security, clients, release, or people.  
5. Assess manager capacity, team interfaces, workspace, tools, budget, recruiting capacity, and twelve-month onboarding ownership.  
6. Create a role scorecard and first-year outcomes. Approve budget and hiring wave/gate before opening requisition.  
7. Review the role if material assumptions change; close or rewrite rather than continuing a stale search.

## **1.3 Employee vs contractor vs partner**

| Choice | Use when | Do not use to | Required controls |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Employee | Ongoing core accountability, management, company knowledge, repeated workload, or protected ownership. | Avoid proper employment obligations or cover temporary uncertainty. | Role scorecard, manager, benefits/compliance, one-year onboarding. |
| Contractor | Time-bounded specialist deliverable or variable capacity with defined independence. | Fill an employee role indefinitely or grant uncontrolled core access. | SOW, classification review, access/data limits, deliverables, offboarding. |
| Agency/partner | Bundled capability, scale, equipment, regional presence, or vendor-managed outcome. | Hide unclear accountability or rights. | Due diligence, contract, service levels, rights/security, transition. |
| Advisor/fractional leader | Early-stage expertise, governance, network, or temporary leadership. | Substitute for an accountable operating owner indefinitely. | Scope, authority, conflicts, confidentiality, succession/exit. |
| Intern/apprentice | Structured learning with supervised, appropriate work. | Provide unsupported critical production capacity. | Curriculum, mentor, safety/access limits, evaluation. |

## **1.4 Hiring priority model**

| Factor | Weight guidance | Question |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Business/production impact | High | Does the role unlock committed revenue, delivery, or strategic proof? |
| Risk reduction | High | Does it remove a security, rights, reliability, continuity, or key-person risk? |
| Capacity constraint | High | Is current qualified capacity persistently above safe utilization? |
| Time to proficiency | Medium-high | How long until the person can operate independently? |
| Scarcity/lead time | Medium | Must recruiting begin early even if start is later? |
| Manager readiness | Gating | Can a manager provide outcomes, feedback, and onboarding? |
| Cash and commitment | Gating | Can the company support total cost through the planning horizon? |
| Alternative availability | Medium | Can process, automation, contractor, or partner meet the need better? |

## **2.2 Job description standard**

* Plain-language mission and why the role exists.  
* Five to eight outcome-oriented responsibilities rather than an exhaustive tool list.  
* Required capabilities separated from trainable or preferred experience.  
* Manager, location/remote expectations, work schedule/on-call/travel where applicable.  
* Compensation range and material benefits where required or company policy supports transparency.  
* Equal opportunity, accommodation, data/privacy, and lawful work-authorization language appropriate to jurisdiction.  
* Accurate description of current company stage, known uncertainty, and role authority.  
* Application process and expected stages without misleading timing guarantees.

## **2.3 Sourcing plan**

| Channel | Best use | Owner action | Quality control |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Employee/professional referrals | Trusted networks and specialized roles. | Provide scorecard and structured process; no guaranteed interview/outcome. | Same evaluation rubric. |
| Targeted outreach | Scarce senior and cross-domain talent. | Personalized reason, role mission, range, process. | Track response and diversity of pool. |
| Professional communities | Unreal, media systems, SRE, AI, post, rights. | Contribute useful information; respect community rules. | Avoid spam and inflated claims. |
| Job platforms | Broad reach and standard roles. | Clear filters and realistic description. | Screen consistently. |
| Agencies/search firms | Urgent, executive, niche, or regional roles. | Approved brief, fee, ownership, candidate data terms. | Performance review and duplicate rules. |
| Education/apprenticeship | Early-career pipeline. | Structured program and manager capacity. | Do not substitute for senior accountability. |
| Contractor/partner conversion | Known performance and ongoing role need. | Reassess classification, compensation, and long-term fit. | Full selection and conflict review. |

## **2.4 Candidate communication standard**

* Acknowledge application or outreach and state the expected process.  
* Provide role mission, reporting line, compensation range, location/work model, material schedule/on-call/travel, and assessment format early.  
* Request only information necessary for hiring; restrict access and retain/delete according to policy and law.  
* Offer accommodations and alternate assessment arrangements without disadvantaging the candidate.  
* Communicate delays and closure promptly; do not leave finalists without status.  
* Interviewers do not make informal promises regarding offer, title, compensation, visa, equity, start date, remote status, or future promotion.  
* Rejections are respectful and consistent; feedback is provided according to company policy and legal guidance.

# **3\. Structured selection process**

## **3.1 Standard hiring stages**

| Stage | Procedure | Output |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 0\. Requisition calibration | Hiring manager, People Ops, interview lead align outcomes, level, range, rubric, questions, work sample, timeline. | Approved scorecard and interview plan. |
| 1\. Application/resume review | Evaluate evidence against minimum requirements using rubric; no unstructured pedigree filter. | Advance/reject with dimension notes. |
| 2\. Recruiter/People screen | Motivation, role understanding, logistics, compensation, work model, candidate questions. | Mutual alignment or closure. |
| 3\. Hiring manager screen | Role outcomes, relevant examples, operating judgment, context, expectations. | Evidence-based score. |
| 4\. Work sample/technical assessment | Representative, bounded, compensated where appropriate; no unpaid production work. | Rubric-scored artifact and debrief. |
| 5\. Structured panel | Independent interviews cover defined competencies without duplication. | Written scores before debrief. |
| 6\. References/verification | With consent; verify scope, outcomes, working style, management needs; lawful checks. | Reference summary and risk notes. |
| 7\. Decision debrief | Compare evidence to scorecard, level, team need, risks; decision owner concludes. | Hire/no-hire/hold with rationale. |
| 8\. Offer and close | Approved compensation/terms, written offer, candidate questions, conditions. | Accepted/declined and learning. |

## **3.2 Interview evidence rules**

* Each interviewer receives assigned competencies and standardized core questions.  
* Interviewers take job-related evidence notes and submit independent scores before group discussion.  
* Use anchored ratings. A number without evidence is not a score.  
* Do not ask questions concerning protected characteristics, medical/family matters, or other unlawful topics.  
* Avoid duplicating the same conversational interview; allocate distinct dimensions and use work evidence.  
* A charismatic or prestigious candidate does not override missing evidence for required outcomes.  
* A concern must identify the scorecard dimension, evidence, risk, and whether onboarding can reasonably close the gap.  
* The final decision includes level, compensation band, references/conditions, onboarding risks, and first manager actions.

## **3.3 Rating scale**

| Rating | Anchor |
| :---- | :---- |
| 1 \- No evidence / material concern | Could not demonstrate required capability or evidence indicates high risk. |
| 2 \- Below role requirement | Partial capability; gap is too large or critical for expected level/time. |
| 3 \- Meets requirement | Credible, specific evidence at the needed level; gaps are manageable. |
| 4 \- Strong | Repeated evidence, sound judgment, teaches or improves the practice. |
| 5 \- Exceptional for scope | Rare depth and leverage beyond requirement; do not inflate role/level without actual need. |

## **3.4 Work-sample standards**

* Representative of real role decisions and artifacts, but not a current company production deliverable.  
* Bounded time and scope; candidate receives context, data, tools, assumptions, evaluation rubric, and accommodation path.  
* Use synthetic or rights-safe fixtures; do not expose client, employee, secret, or production information.  
* Assess reasoning, tradeoffs, verification, communication, and handling of uncertainty—not only polished output.  
* For senior roles, include system/organizational judgment and a review/debrief, not merely coding speed.  
* Store candidate work securely and delete/retain according to policy; do not reuse without explicit agreement.

## **3.5 Role-specific work samples**

| Role family | Sample | Scored dimensions |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Backend/data | Design and test idempotent render job creation and worker lease. | Transactions, schema, failure handling, tests, explanation. |
| SRE/operations | Create incident/recovery plan for NAS or Rafa outage. | Prioritization, RPO/RTO, security, communication, validation. |
| Unreal/render | Automate atomic camera/render fixture from typed config. | UE knowledge, reproducibility, output checks, debugging. |
| Workflow automation | Model approval plus async provider lifecycle. | Durability, state, idempotency, user experience, errors. |
| AI integration | Normalize two fictional providers and define evaluation. | Abstraction, unknowns, quality, cost, security. |
| Media/post | Diagnose media fixture and build conformant timeline/export. | Timecode, codec/audio/QC, evidence. |
| Producer | Plan a ten-shot pilot with gates, dependencies, budget, and change. | Scope, critical path, communication, risk, economics. |
| Sales/client success | Qualify a scenario and lead a scope/expectation conversation. | Discovery, honesty, value, commercial judgment. |
| People/operations | Build first-month onboarding for a technical hire with constrained resources. | Planning, inclusion, controls, manager enablement. |
| Executive leader | Present a staged decision on scaling the company after mixed pilot results. | Strategy, finance, people, risk, clarity. |

## **3.6 Reference-check procedure**

8. Obtain candidate consent and agree references appropriate to role and jurisdiction.  
9. Verify reference identity and relationship; do not rely solely on unverified personal contact information.  
10. Ask about responsibilities, outcomes, quality, collaboration, judgment, reliability, management environment, strengths, and development needs.  
11. Ask what conditions enabled the candidate to perform and whether the reference would work with them again, where lawful.  
12. Distinguish fact, opinion, and inability to disclose; document neutrally.  
13. Escalate material inconsistencies to People Ops and hiring manager; allow candidate clarification.

# **4\. Offer, preboarding, readiness, and compliance**

## **4.1 Offer approval**

| Element | Required approval/check |
| :---- | :---- |
| Role/level/title | Matches authorized scorecard and organization design. |
| Compensation | Within approved range/equity philosophy and internal consistency; exceptions documented. |
| Location/work model | Tax, employment, security, equipment, and manager support feasible. |
| Start date | Manager, team, equipment, access, payroll, workspace, and onboarding readiness. |
| Conditions | References, background/work authorization, confidentiality/IP, policy acknowledgements as lawful. |
| Signatory | Authorized company representative; no informal manager commitment. |
| Candidate communication | Written offer and clear expiration/decision process; respectful questions and negotiation. |

## **4.2 Preboarding timeline**

| Timing | People Ops | Manager | IT/Ops | Employee |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Offer accepted | Create employee record/checklist; conditions and forms. | Confirm scorecard, start plan, buddy. | Receive readiness ticket. | Submit required lawful information. |
| 2-4 weeks before | Payroll/benefits/handbook preparation. | Build 30/60/90 and first assignments. | Order/provision device, software, access, desk/shipping. | Review welcome and logistics. |
| 1 week before | Confirm documents and orientation. | Send schedule, team context, first-week expectations. | Run readiness validation; ship/prepare equipment. | Confirm receipt/travel/time zone/accommodation. |
| 1 business day before | Final active status check. | Confirm meetings and welcome. | Identity activation timing, support contact, physical access. | Do not access before authorized start. |
| Start day | Employment orientation and acknowledgements. | Welcome, context, role, team. | Secure first login and device/access check. | Complete setup and orientation. |

## **4.3 Manager preboarding deliverables**

* Final role scorecard and one-year outcomes.  
* Thirty-, sixty-, and ninety-day plan with real but safe assignments.  
* Twelve-month learning and certification map for the role.  
* Named buddy, mentor/functional experts, and their expectations.  
* Stakeholder map, meeting cadence, team norms, decision rights, and escalation.  
* Access/software/workspace profile reviewed for least privilege.  
* First two weeks scheduled with focus time; avoid meeting overload.  
* Definition of early success and known onboarding risks or accommodations.

## **4.4 Day-one readiness gate**

## **5.3 Days 8-30: orientation to contribution**

* Complete mandatory company, security, rights/privacy, safety, and role baseline training.  
* Demonstrate development/sandbox workflow, documentation, request, change, support, and incident reporting.  
* Shadow upstream and downstream work so the employee understands the complete production lifecycle.  
* Deliver at least one low-risk, production-relevant assignment through normal review and evidence standards.  
* Establish one-on-one cadence, working agreement, development goals, and feedback preference.  
* Manager confirms role scope, level, workload, access, and onboarding risks; corrects gaps rather than silently lowering expectations.

### **30-day checkpoint**

| Dimension | Evidence/questions | Decision |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Readiness | Device, access, software, workspace, support, required forms complete? | Close gaps. |
| Understanding | Can employee explain mission, offers, lifecycle, role, stakeholders, policies? | Reinforce curriculum. |
| Execution | Did first assignment meet quality, review, and documentation standards? | Adjust coaching/scope. |
| Behavior | Is communication, reliability, curiosity, inclusion, and safety aligned? | Recognize or address. |
| Well-being/workload | Is pace sustainable and accommodation/support sufficient? | Modify conditions. |
| Plan | Are 60/90 and year goals still correct? | Confirm or revise in writing. |

## **5.4 Days 31-60: supervised ownership**

* Own a defined component, workflow, project workstream, customer/production task, or operational service under supervision.  
* Plan work, identify dependencies/risks, estimate, communicate status, and close evidence using company systems.  
* Complete role-specific intermediate training and simulation; production access remains scoped to demonstrated need.  
* Participate in review, incident/game-day, project gate, or retrospective appropriate to role.  
* Give and receive specific feedback; manager observes real work rather than relying on self-report.  
* Identify one documentation or process gap and improve it through approved change.

### **60-day checkpoint**

Manager assesses output quality, pace, judgment, collaboration, learning, documentation, security/rights behavior, and support needed. Material gaps receive a clear plan with expected evidence and review date. Role mismatch is addressed early and respectfully rather than postponed until the end of probation.

## **5.5 Days 61-90: role proficiency gate**

* Deliver a meaningful role outcome with normal review and limited prompting.  
* Demonstrate safe use of approved systems and correct escalation when uncertain.  
* Show understanding of upstream/downstream impact, customer/creative intent, cost, quality, and operational evidence.  
* Complete the initial role certification or explicitly document remaining restrictions.  
* Propose next-quarter outcomes and a six-to-twelve-month development plan.  
* Participate in a cross-functional review and explain tradeoffs clearly.

### **90-day proficiency decision**

| Decision | Meaning | Action |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| On track / certify initial scope | Meets expected initial proficiency and behavior. | Expand normal role scope; continue development. |
| On track with specific gaps | Core fit is sound; bounded gaps require planned support. | Written gap plan, owner, date, evidence. |
| Role/scope adjustment | Strengths fit a different scope or level and business can support it. | Formal change with People/Finance approval. |
| Performance concern | Required outcomes or conduct materially below expectation. | Follow lawful performance process; clarity, support, review. |
| End employment/engagement | Role fit or requirements not met and alternatives are not appropriate. | People/legal process and secure offboarding. |

## **5.6 Months 4-6: independent contribution**

| Dimension | Expectation | Evidence |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Role ownership | Own normal-scope work or service with manager review proportional to risk. | Delivered outcome and stakeholder feedback. |
| Cross-functional fluency | Understand and improve one interface outside immediate team. | Handoff improvement. |
| Quality and operations | Participate in support, release, incident, QC, or customer cycle appropriate to role. | Operational evidence. |
| Business understanding | Connect work to customer value, cost, schedule, quality, rights, and strategy. | Quarterly outcome narrative. |
| Development | Complete intermediate curriculum/certification and practice feedback/coaching. | Learning evidence. |
| Improvement | Close one meaningful process, reliability, cost, quality, or knowledge improvement. | Measured before/after. |

### **Six-month review**

The six-month review evaluates role outcomes, proficiency, operating behavior, collaboration, reliability, development, workload, engagement, and future scope. It is not a surprise event; it summarizes regular feedback. The manager calibrates with appropriate peers and People Ops and confirms goals for months seven through twelve.

## **5.7 Months 7-9: leverage and broader ownership**

* Own a larger workstream, service objective, client/production milestone, or operational improvement.  
* Mentor or assist a newer colleague, document a practice, or teach a role-relevant session.  
* Use metrics and evidence to identify root causes, not only complete assigned tasks.  
* Demonstrate judgment about when to act independently, consult, escalate, or stop work.  
* Contribute to quarterly planning, capacity, risk, roadmap, or quality review.  
* Complete advanced or production-specific certification for the role where required.

### **Nine-month checkpoint**

Manager and employee compare first-year outcomes to evidence, identify remaining gaps, discuss career direction and motivation, and select the capstone outcome for the final quarter. Retention risks, workload, compensation concerns, management support, and role ambiguity are addressed before the annual review.

## **5.8 Months 10-12: certification and next-year plan**

* Deliver a capstone outcome that demonstrates role-level ownership and cross-functional value.  
* Complete first-year role certification and any production, privileged, safety, security, or rights authorization.  
* Create or materially improve durable documentation, training, automation, template, asset, or process for successors.  
* Provide structured feedback on onboarding and recommend changes supported by evidence.  
* Review decision rights and access; remove temporary onboarding permissions and confirm appropriate standing access.  
* Agree next-year outcomes, development plan, career direction, and contribution to team succession/mentoring.

# **8\. Manager, buddy, mentor, and employee responsibilities**

## **8.1 Manager**

* Provide role clarity, outcomes, priorities, context, decision rights, resources, and psychological safety to ask questions or stop unsafe work.  
* Prepare the team and systems before start; do not delegate all onboarding to People Ops or the buddy.  
* Meet frequently during the first month, weekly through at least ninety days, and regularly thereafter.  
* Observe real work, give timely specific feedback, recognize progress, and document material concerns.  
* Adjust scope and training based on evidence while preserving role requirements and fairness.  
* Connect employee to customers, production lifecycle, adjacent functions, and business economics.  
* Own 30/60/90/180/270/365 checkpoints and close onboarding actions.

## **8.2 Buddy**

* Help with practical navigation, team norms, terminology, tools, and informal questions.  
* Schedule regular contact during the first month and decreasing cadence through ninety days.  
* Model safe, inclusive, documented work; never provide unauthorized access or override manager/policy.  
* Notice friction and escalate systemic onboarding gaps to manager/People Ops.  
* The buddy is not the employee's performance evaluator or substitute manager.

## **8.3 Functional mentor/certifier**

* Provide curriculum, examples, practice, review, and technical/functional feedback.  
* Use published certification rubric and evidence; separate coaching from final certification where practical.  
* Keep training current with approved systems and procedures.  
* Escalate unsafe, rights-ineligible, insecure, or unsupported practice immediately.

## **8.4 Employee**

* Complete required preparation, training, documentation, and evidence; ask when context or authority is unclear.  
* Use approved systems, protect information/assets, follow safety/security/rights controls, and report incidents promptly.  
* Prepare for one-on-ones and checkpoints with outcomes, learning, blockers, feedback, and next steps.  
* Seek and act on feedback; give respectful feedback on management and onboarding.  
* Do not exceed authorized access or approval rights; escalate rather than improvising high-risk decisions.  
* Contribute durable knowledge and help improve the system as proficiency grows.

## **8.5 One-on-one cadence**

| Period | Cadence | Agenda |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Week 1 | Daily brief check-ins plus end-of-week review. | Setup, understanding, energy, questions, next day. |
| Weeks 2-4 | At least twice weekly. | Assignment, feedback, stakeholders, learning, blockers. |
| Months 2-3 | Weekly. | Outcomes, quality, judgment, workload, development, 60/90. |
| Months 4-6 | Weekly or biweekly based on role and need. | Ownership, cross-functional work, six-month review. |
| Months 7-12 | At least biweekly; monthly only by mature agreement. | Outcomes, career, capstone, engagement, next-year plan. |

# **9\. Performance, feedback, development, inclusion, and retention**

## **9.1 Feedback standard**

* Timely: close enough to the event for learning and correction.  
* Specific: describe observed behavior/output and context, not personality labels.  
* Impact-linked: explain effect on customer, team, quality, cost, schedule, risk, or trust.  
* Actionable: clarify expected behavior and support; invite perspective.  
* Balanced: recognize effective behavior and address gaps; do not save material issues for formal review.  
* Documented appropriately: routine coaching may be informal; material expectations/concerns require written clarity under People policy.

## **9.2 Performance assessment dimensions**

| Dimension | Questions |
| :---- | :---- |
| Outcomes | Did the employee deliver agreed results at expected quality, scope, and level? |
| Craft/functional capability | Are methods technically/operationally sound and improving? |
| Judgment | Do they make appropriate decisions, manage uncertainty, and escalate correctly? |
| Ownership/reliability | Do they plan, communicate, follow through, and protect commitments? |
| Collaboration/inclusion | Do they improve team effectiveness, respect others, and handle conflict? |
| Customer/creative/business | Do they understand value, cost, schedule, quality, and company strategy? |
| Safety/security/rights | Do they protect people, data, assets, credentials, and legal obligations? |
| Learning/leverage | Do they learn, document, automate, teach, and make future work easier? |

## **9.4 Inclusion and accommodation**

* Provide accessible recruiting and onboarding materials and a clear accommodation request path.

# **10\. Special employment lifecycle procedures**

## **10.1 Internal transfer**

14. New manager creates role scorecard and business case; current manager and People Ops review transition and performance context.  
15. Reassess compensation/level, conflicts, location, equipment, software, access, and replacement/knowledge transfer.  
16. Create transfer onboarding plan with 30/60/90 outcomes and role certification deltas.  
17. Remove obsolete access and responsibilities; transfer ownership and communicate interfaces.  
18. Run 30/60/90 transfer checkpoints; do not assume company tenure equals new-role proficiency.

## **10.2 Contractor onboarding**

19. Validate classification and signed SOW, confidentiality/IP, data/security/rights, insurance, term, deliverables, and manager.  
20. Provide only necessary identity, device/access, project data, and facility access; set automatic expiration.  
21. Complete security, privacy, rights, safety, and project training appropriate to access.  
22. Define deliverable acceptance, communication, invoicing, change, support, ownership, and offboarding.  
23. Review access and performance at least monthly for critical engagements; offboard immediately at end.

## **10.3 Performance concern**

Managers must involve People Ops and follow applicable law and company policy. The company should clarify the required outcome or conduct, specific evidence, impact, prior feedback, support, reasonable review period, and consequences. Safety, harassment, fraud, security, or rights issues may require different immediate action. Do not improvise disciplinary processes in chat or hide concerns until an annual review.

## **10.4 Offboarding**

| Domain | Action | Owner |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Authorization | Confirmed last working time, reason/process owner, communication, legal/HR requirements. | People Ops |
| Access | Disable identity and sessions; revoke keys/tokens/badges; remove groups; rotate shared secrets. | IT/Security |
| Assets | Return device, peripherals, keys, media, documents; inspect, inventory, wipe/redeploy. | IT/Ops |
| Ownership | Transfer repositories, workflows, documents, accounts, vendors, projects, approvals, contacts. | Manager |
| Data/records | Preserve required company/legal records; delete personal/local copies; honor legal hold. | Legal/People/Data |

| Communication | Appropriate team/client/vendor communication and confidentiality reminder. | Manager/Comms |
| Post-close | Verify access logs, returned assets, ownership, open actions; review regrettable attrition and systemic causes. | People/Ops |

# **14\. Adoption checklist**

* Appoint Head of People/People Operations owner and require every manager to complete manager onboarding before hiring.  
* Authorize roles only through the workforce planning and role scorecard process.  
* Use structured evidence and calibrated work samples for selection; remove unstructured duplicate interviews.  
* Integrate People, manager, IT, security, facilities, finance, and legal readiness before start.  
* Run onboarding for the full first year with documented checkpoints, role certifications, employee feedback, and next-year plan.  
* Audit outcomes quarterly and revise curriculum when architecture, systems, policies, markets, or role requirements change.
