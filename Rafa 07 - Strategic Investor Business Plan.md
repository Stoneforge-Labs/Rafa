# **Cinematic/Virtual Production architecture**

| Architecture doctrine: Rafa is a software-defined cinematic and virtual-production company. Unreal Engine is treated as a deterministic scene-construction and offline-rendering service inside a governed production factory. The company is not building a game, a gameplay loop, or a consumer game client. |
| :---- |

## **Why the category remains under-automated**

The opportunity exists because the required skill set spans disciplines that normally live in separate organizations. Cinematographers, character artists, and animators understand visual quality but may not design durable orchestration, database schemas, APIs, CI/CD, security boundaries, or failure recovery. Software and infrastructure engineers understand those controls but may not understand camera language, Sequencer, Control Rig, MetaHuman, Chaos, timing, and editorial acceptance. Rafa is designed around the deliberate merger of those disciplines.

* Art-versus-infrastructure gap: creative practitioners and distributed-systems engineers rarely share one production operating model.  
* Game-engine mindset trap: most teams treat Unreal as an interactive editor or game runtime instead of a scriptable cinematic rendering service.  
* Upfront systems cost: reliable automation requires governed data, private render control, storage throughput, GPU capacity, reproducible templates, monitoring, and recovery before scale.  
* Artistic-control misconception: automation does not remove control; it encodes approved creative intent into versioned parameters, assets, constraints, and review gates.  
* Provider volatility: generative providers change models, policies, pricing, quotas, and formats, so the company must own the normalized contract and workflow state.

## **Cinematic implementation patterns**

| Pattern | Required design | Business consequence |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Sequencer as source of truth | Each shot is represented by a governed Level Sequence or sequence configuration generated from the approved manifest. | Shot state becomes versionable, reproducible, auditable, and portable across render nodes. |
| Spawnables where appropriate | Shot-scoped actors and props are spawned by Sequencer rather than left permanently resident in the level. | Reduces state leakage and memory accumulation across long unattended queues; requires benchmark validation per asset type. |
| Offline deterministic evaluation | Chaos, animation, cloth, hair, particles, and camera are evaluated under frame-controlled rendering rather than real-time gameplay assumptions. | Prioritizes repeatability and quality over interactive frame rate. |
| Control Rig for procedural performance | Breathing, muscle tension, joint offsets, facial controls, and procedural transforms live in Control Rig or validated native systems, not heavyweight per-frame Blueprint logic. | Preserves creative programmability while reducing CPU overhead and rewrite risk. |
| Subsystem ingestion model | A custom Editor/Engine Subsystem receives validated manifests and sets global production state; scene actors do not poll external systems. | Centralizes validation, reduces hidden state, and keeps actors focused on rendering behavior. |
| C++ for heavy array math | Large vector, weather, transform, facial, and simulation arrays are processed in native code or optimized engine facilities and exposed through narrow interfaces. | Improves throughput, predictability, testability, and maintainability. |
| No production Event Tick abuse | Expensive I/O, database access, weather queries, and large loops do not execute every frame in general Blueprints. | Avoids CPU bottlenecks that waste GPU capacity and make queue economics unpredictable. |
| MRQ warm-up and pre-roll | Engine and render warm-up frames settle physics, cloth, hair, particles, exposure, and caches before recorded frame one. | Prevents first-frame defects and reduces manual rework. |
| Private edge control | Kestra calls an authenticated edge gateway; Unreal Remote Control and workstation administration are never public endpoints. | Enables enterprise security, auditability, and insurability. |

## **Strategic IP and data boundaries**

* Rafa background IP includes platform code, architecture, generic workflows, templates, adapters, QC methods, evaluation methods, documentation, and improvements not uniquely derived from partner confidential information.  
* Partner background IP includes its content, brands, characters, source assets, contracts, data, internal systems, and pre-existing tools.  
* Project deliverables and custom integration ownership must be defined by category. Rafa should retain reusable generic improvements while granting the partner broad use of paid deliverables in the agreed field.  
* No partner content, performer identity, voice, project data, or confidential prompts become generalized training data without explicit separate written permission, purpose, retention, and benefit allocation.  
* Benchmark and aggregate operational data may be used only if contractually permitted, sufficiently de-identified, non-confidential, and unable to expose partner production plans or content.  
* Any exclusivity must be limited by field, territory, duration, minimum commitment, performance, termination, and carve-outs; broad company-level exclusivity is generally incompatible with venture value.

# **Investment structure, governance, and reserved rights**

## **Preferred structure: minority equity plus separate commercial agreement**

* Equity documents govern ownership, board, information rights, protective provisions, transfer, future financing, and exit.  
* Commercial documents govern implementation, platform, production, capacity, support, service levels, roadmap process, data, IP, security, rights, pricing, and termination.  
* The partner receives no implied exclusivity, perpetual free services, broad IP license, generalized training rights, or customer veto through the equity investment.  
* Material commercial advantages require minimum commitments, performance, time limits, and termination/step-down rights.  
* If the partner supplies cloud credits, hardware, data, content, distribution, or personnel, cash-equivalent value and restrictions must be documented separately.

## **Illustrative strategic structures**

| Structure | Advantages | Risks / controls |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Minority preferred equity \+ MSA | Clean company capitalization, diversified growth, separately measurable commercial value. | Control board rights and commercial dependence; no hidden exclusivity. |

| Data and training | No implicit secondary use; explicit purpose, eligibility, security, retention, deletion, benefit and publication terms. |
| Board conflicts | Director duties, recusals, information limits, related-party approval and clean-team protocols. |
| Change of control | Commercial continuity, assignment, data return/deletion, transition support, license survival as negotiated. |
| Termination | Orderly wind-down, in-flight productions, service data, asset return, export, payment, support and knowledge transfer. |
| Downside financing | Objective milestone/cure, bridge mechanics, no punitive IP transfer, board process and alternative-financing freedom. |
| Acquisition path | Any option/ROFN is narrow and timed; independent board approval and fairness/valuation process. |

# **Strategic term-sheet discussion framework**

| Term area | Rafa preferred position | Negotiation questions |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Security and amount | Minority preferred equity with sufficient cash runway; commercial commitments separate. | What is cash vs credits/in-kind? What milestone capital is committed? |
| Board | One strategic seat or observer appropriate to ownership; independent director; conflict rules. | What information and committees are necessary? How are competitive matters handled? |
| Information rights | Standard financial/company information with confidentiality and competitive clean-team limits. | Can the partner access customer, roadmap, provider or pricing detail beyond investment need? |
| Commercial minimum | Multi-year minimum covering platform, support, capacity and/or production. | What volume, timing, forecast, true-up and termination apply? |
| Exclusivity | None by default; narrow and paid if essential. | Field, territory, duration, commitment, performance, exceptions, sunset? |
| IP and license | Rafa retains platform/background/generic improvements; partner receives agreed use rights. | Who owns custom integration, project assets, jointly developed inventions? |
| Data/training | No generalized training right; separate written, rights-cleared program. | Purpose, scope, security, model ownership, benefit, deletion, audit? |
| ROFN/option | Avoid or strictly limit; no perpetual overhang. | Duration, scope, process, valuation, matching, standstill and termination? |
| Most-favored terms | Limited to genuinely comparable commercial scope and time. | Does it block future packaging or customers? |
| Exit/termination | Operational continuity, export, transition, support, payment and confidentiality. | What happens to in-flight work, dedicated equipment, data and licenses? |
